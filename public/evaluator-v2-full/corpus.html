01 · Intelligence

AI is getting better at imagining what could happen next.

But imagining, planning, understanding a situation, acting, and knowing what actually happened are different abilities.

Intelligence is doing more than one job.

We use nine working dimensions to keep those jobs separate. The point is practical: a system can be brilliant at generating possibilities and still need a different way to handle authority, consequences, evidence, and changing situations.

GenerativeProduces possibilities, plans, hypotheses, designs, and candidate futures.
ConceptualForms, transforms, and relates abstractions.
SituationalUnderstands what is true, relevant, and constrained now.
RelationalRepresents identity, authority, obligation, dependency, and exposure between things.
TemporalReasons over sequence, expiry, revocation, finality, history, and changing state.
ConsequentialRepresents how possible actions alter reachable future states and who bears effects.
EvidentiaryDistinguishes assertion, execution, observation, contradiction, uncertainty, and evidence.
OrganizationalCoordinates consequence across people, agents, roles, institutions, and workflows.
ReflectiveLets evidence about prior action alter what becomes possible or legitimate next.
A mind can be imaginative without being wise, informed without being authorized, and confident without being right. AI makes those old distinctions computationally urgent.
Getting better at creating possibilities does not automatically make a system better at deciding what should become real.

That gap is where this work begins.

A familiar example

A navigation app may know every road to your destination. That does not mean every road is open to you right now. Some are closed, some require permission, some are unsafe, and some stop being available as conditions change. Intelligence can know the routes without owning the decision about which route may be taken.

02 · Consequence

What an AI can do is bigger than what it should be allowed to make happen.

Once AI can act through tools and systems, we need a way to keep track of that difference as the situation changes.

A boundary around what can become real.

The technical name is Consequential Reachability.

In everyday terms: of all the things the system could make happen from here, which ones are actually allowed to become real now?

ℛC(St) ⊆ ℛT(St)
Capability asks: “Can it?”
Consequential reachability asks: “May this happen now?”

The machine keeps that boundary current by carrying forward who is acting, whose authority matters, what limits apply, what is already at risk, what other things depend on this action, how much time has passed, and what the evidence says actually happened.

Think of it like a credit card—but more general

Your card may be technically capable of sending a payment. Whether a particular payment should go through depends on who is using it, the amount, available capacity, fraud signals, timing, and what already happened. VALIS generalizes that kind of changing boundary beyond money to consequential actions themselves.

03 · Computation

The rules cannot disappear just because the situation changes.

If an AI acts, the relationships that made that action acceptable still matter afterward: who authorized it, what was allowed, what changed, and what remains uncertain.

Decide. Observe. Learn what happened. Decide again.

We call that process Recursive Relational Settlement. A proposed action is checked against the current situation. What actually happens becomes evidence for the next decision.

PROPOSAL → SETTLEMENT → RESERVATION → RELEASE → EXECUTION → EFFECT → OBSERVATION → RECONCILIATION → EVIDENCE → NEW SETTLED STATE
Asking is not acting. Being capable is not being authorized. What was supposed to happen is not always what actually happened. Not knowing is not permission.

When the machine does not know what happened, it does not quietly assume everything is fine. Uncertainty changes what can safely happen next.

Why uncertainty matters

If you press “send” on a bank transfer and the screen freezes, you do not safely solve the problem by pressing “send” five more times. First you need to know whether the first transfer happened. The same principle matters when autonomous systems can cause real effects.

04 · Machine

We built a computer around that question.

Its job is to keep track of the changing boundary between what a connected system could make happen and what it is actually allowed to make happen.

VALIS-KORE is the working machine.

We call the architecture a Autonomous Relational Computer because it computes over relationships and consequences as they change—not just over data.

AI can keep generating possibilities. The machine keeps deciding which of them may become real.

VALIS-KORE is where the question became something we could actually run, test, and try to break.

Another way to picture it

A compiler turns human-written code into something a machine can execute. VALIS does something different but analogous in spirit: it takes relationships that normally live in policy, intention, authority, and context and makes them part of the computation that determines what can happen next.

05 · Evidence

Does it actually hold up?

You should not have to take our word for it. The important claims can be inspected, tested, challenged, and reopened when evidence contradicts them.

Every important claim has a way to fail.

For each major claim, we show what it means, where it applies, what would prove it wrong, how we tested it, what happened, and what is still unfinished.

The standard is simple

If we say the machine will not silently restore capacity when an outcome is unknown, there must be a test that deliberately creates that uncertainty and checks what the machine does. In our testing, deliberately broken UNKNOWN-effect behavior was detected. That is more useful than asking someone to trust the claim.

518 typed settled states × 8 canonical proposals · 50,000 deterministic fuzz runs · 960/960 differential matches · broken UNKNOWN semantics detected.

Some questions are settled inside the model. Others still need production evidence, real-world deployment, or independent reproduction. We keep those boundaries visible instead of blending them into one pass/fail claim.

06 · Meaning

More intelligence should mean more human possibility—not less human agency.

The goal is not to make AI less capable. It is to keep capability from quietly becoming the power to decide what becomes real.

The human analogy

We already understand this distinction in ordinary life. A talented surgeon, pilot, engineer, attorney, or financial adviser can know far more than the person they serve. Expertise gives them capability; it does not automatically give them unlimited authority. Powerful AI creates the same question at machine speed and scale.

The question became the machine.

We asked who gets to determine what becomes real, then built that distinction into the way the computer works.

Intelligence can expand possibility without owning consequence.

The executable system, its reachable futures, releases, refusals, evidence, failures, corrections, and human decisions are the material of the inquiry.

MECHANISM FALSIFICATION PROGRAM

Produce a counterexample to the mechanism itself.

A difficult proposal is not, by itself, a break. A proposer remains inside settlement. The stronger challenge is to demonstrate a transition in which the settlement relation violates a declared invariant, an implementation diverges from that relation, or consequence escapes settlement entirely.

01 · SEMANTIC CHALLENGE

Break an invariant

Produce a representable settled-state / proposal trace for which settlement violates a declared invariant.

Success: a reproducible counterexample to the specified transition semantics.

02 · CONFORMANCE CHALLENGE

Make implementation disagree

Produce an execution in which an implementation returns a result inconsistent with the specification.

Success: canonical inputs produce a result the declared semantics do not permit.

03 · MEDIATION CHALLENGE

Escape settlement

Identify a consequentially reachable path that alters governed state without a valid settlement and bound release.

Success: consequence occurs outside the settlement boundary.

What does not count as a break?

A novel, hostile, malformed, or surprising proposal that is nevertheless classified by the declared settlement semantics without violating an invariant. Proposal novelty does not itself confer authority.

CONTINUITY & CLOSURE PROTOCOL

Review creates evidence. It does not grant permission to continue.

Established results remain established within their declared scope unless contradictory evidence reopens them. Unresolved questions become bounded work packages with explicit closure conditions. Accepted failures become versioned corrections, regression tests, and stronger subsequent releases.

Closed means closed within scope.

Opinion, silence, category disagreement, or reviewer preference does not change status. Reopening requires contradictory evidence material to the stated scope.

OPEN becomes executable work.

Every open item states the missing evidence, required test or artifact, and exact closure condition.

External review produces evidence.

Reviewers may challenge, reproduce, falsify, correct, or extend the record. They are not permission authorities for continuation.

No reviewer veto.

A reviewer can reopen a claim by reproducible evidence, not by status, preference, or institutional prestige.

No single commercial dependency gate.

Partners, insurers, investors, platforms, and labs may accelerate a track, but no single counterparty is prerequisite to the architecture, evaluator, evidence program, or next release.

Failure strengthens the next version.

Accepted defects receive an ID, affected scope, correction, regression test, evidence update, and versioned release record.
